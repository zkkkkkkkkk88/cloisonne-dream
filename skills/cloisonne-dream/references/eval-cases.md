# Evaluation Cases v0.4

Use these cases as visual regression checks after any skill change.

## Global v0.4 pass criteria

Every output must satisfy all of the following:

- source subject count, pose, viewpoint, major silhouette, object placement, and scene identity remain traceable;
- no new major scene subject is invented;
- the image is visibly non-photographic and reads as cloisonne enamel rather than stained glass or a gold-outline filter;
- the composition contains all three structural zones when the source permits them: **Identity Cells**, **Flow Cells**, and **Quiet Cells**;
- line density is intentionally uneven: Identity is readable, Flow is richest, Quiet is calmer but still finished;
- at least one meaningful **Gold-Line Continuity** path links existing source forms;
- source-derived colors remain recognizable while receiving a luminous enamel uplift;
- micro-detail is compressed into enamel structure rather than reproduced as noise;
- existing surfaces may become more ornate, intricate, and jewel-like without changing scene logic;
- a controlled decorative motif layer may be present when it improves composition;
- any motif remains secondary to the source subject and varies according to composition rather than a fixed template;
- the finished image should feel brighter, more polished, more decorative, and more collectible than a literal material conversion.

## Known failure modes

Reject or regenerate when:
- the same decorative sun appears in the same corner across unrelated images;
- a sun, moon, star field, cloud band, snowflake, floral emblem, or bird motif becomes the main focal point;
- several large decorative symbols compete with the subject;
- a new dragon, person, building, vehicle, or major prop is invented;
- motifs cover the face, dragon head, important hand, prop, or landmark;
- every object receives the same dense gold outline;
- the result looks like generic stained glass;
- a generic blue-white-gold palette replaces source identity;
- dark source values are kept so literally that the result becomes muddy or visually flat;
- decorative richness is reduced so aggressively that the result is faithful but underwhelming;
- random filigree ignores source geometry and composition.

## Case 1: Character in a garden

Source characteristics:
- single seated character;
- parasol;
- dense green garden;
- wooden railing;
- white/lavender clothing.

Pass criteria:
- face and parasol remain recognizable Identity Cells;
- hair and clothing folds become richer Flow Cells with directional segmentation;
- distant foliage and open background become calmer Quiet Cells;
- clothing and hair may gain more intricate internal enamel design;
- green garden identity remains intact;
- overall image becomes cleaner and more luminous than the source;
- one restrained moon disc, floral emblem, cloud band, or similar motif may be used only if negative space and mood support it;
- no motif may cover the character or parasol.

## Case 2: White dragon over fantasy architecture

Source characteristics:
- one large white dragon;
- pale castle/city below;
- blue daytime sky;
- pink-tinted wing membranes.

Pass criteria:
- dragon head, wing silhouette, and city mass remain immediately traceable;
- dragon scales and wing membranes form high-density Flow Cells;
- scales may become more jewel-like, layered, and decorative while staying on the original dragon;
- sky remains a lower-density Quiet Cell field;
- white, blue, and pink identity remains recognizable but more luminous;
- one elegant sun disc or restrained celestial motif cluster is allowed when placed in genuine open sky and kept secondary;
- no second dragon, new tower group, or major scene object may be introduced.

## Case 3: Ice queen with dragon

Source characteristics:
- standing woman in icy dress;
- staff;
- large dark icy dragon behind her;
- dark blue environment.

Pass criteria:
- woman, staff, and dragon all remain with original hierarchy;
- face, staff head, and dragon head remain Identity Cells;
- dress, hair, dragon scales, wings, and ice structures carry the richest Flow Cell segmentation;
- dark environment remains cool but is lifted into richer sapphire, cobalt, lapis, pearl-white, and icy translucent values where appropriate;
- dress and dragon may receive bold internal enamel redesign while their silhouettes and roles remain unchanged;
- crystalline stars, snowflake emblems, or one restrained celestial ornament are allowed when kept secondary;
- the final image must not feel duller or flatter than earlier strong Cloisonne Dream examples;
- no second dragon or new narrative object may appear.

## Case 4: Woman on cloud bridge toward bright castle

Source characteristics:
- woman seen in profile/back-three-quarter view;
- long pale dress and veil;
- winding elevated bridge;
- pale castle in distance;
- clouds dominate environment;
- bright sunlight already exists in source.

Pass criteria:
- bridge path, woman placement, castle, clouds, and existing bright light remain recognizable;
- dress, veil, bridge path, and existing cloud forms become elegant Flow Cells;
- distant cloud fields remain comparatively quiet;
- existing sunlight may be restyled because it is already present;
- pale materials become luminous rather than gray or washed out;
- an added motif is unnecessary unless it clearly improves balance; do not duplicate the existing sun.

## Failure trigger

Regenerate or tighten constraints if:
- a new major scene object appears;
- the source pose, viewpoint, count, or structure changes materially;
- a decorative motif dominates the composition;
- motif placement feels repetitive or template-driven;
- the result is darker, muddier, or visually flatter than expected;
- Flow Cells are too plain to carry the style's luxury;
- the result is technically faithful but visibly less beautiful than a previous successful output;
- the output fails the **scene-faithful + surface-bold + luminous + controlled-motif** v0.4 grammar.
