# Cloisonne Dream Style Rules

## 1. Source Content Has Priority

This is a medium-conversion skill, not a scene-redesign skill.

When style and content conflict, source content wins.

Preserve:

- subject count
- pose
- viewpoint
- framing
- object placement
- architecture layout
- background identity
- major silhouette
- important color identity

## 2. Forbidden Hallucinated Decorations

A common failure mode is the model inventing decorative symbols simply because they are associated with traditional enamel, fantasy illustration, or Chinese decorative art.

Strictly prohibit spontaneous additions such as:

- decorative suns
- golden circular discs
- moons
- stars
- flying birds
- stylized clouds
- auspicious cloud motifs
- flowers
- mountains
- lanterns
- jewels
- snowflake motifs
- celestial patterns
- extra buildings
- extra creatures
- extra vegetation

Exception: the element may be rendered if it genuinely exists in the source image or the user explicitly asks for it.

## 3. Medium Conversion

Convert:

- photographic texture -> enamel color fields
- natural edges -> elegant metal partition lines
- fine random detail -> simplified controlled shapes
- surface noise -> polished glazed material
- realistic pores -> clean illustrated skin treatment
- messy foliage detail -> grouped enamel leaf masses

Do not convert:

- subject identity
- composition
- pose
- object count
- environment identity
- camera viewpoint
- spatial relationships

## 4. Gold-Line Discipline

Gold is structural, not a blanket effect.

Use gold or warm brass mainly for:

- cell boundaries
- contour partitions
- selected garment structure
- architectural edges
- existing ornament details

Avoid:

- global gold wash
- excessive glitter
- random gold filigree
- invented jewelry or decoration
- gold outlines on every tiny texture fragment

## 5. Detail Compression

The style should simplify noisy photo detail into readable enamel cells.

Good simplification:

- one tree canopy -> several grouped green cells
- long hair -> flowing segmented ribbons
- stone wall -> larger geometric panels
- dragon scales -> readable repeated enamel units

Bad simplification:

- deleting important objects
- changing pose
- flattening faces beyond recognition
- replacing background identity

## 6. Source-Aware Color

Do not force all images into the same blue-white-gold palette.

Retain the source's dominant hue relationships wherever possible and reinterpret them as enamel materials.

Examples:

- green field stays predominantly green
- lavender clothing stays lavender/white
- red roof remains recognizably red
- black dragon remains dark if that is identity-defining

## 7. No Photography Residue

The output should not resemble a filtered photo.

Remove or avoid:

- sensor noise
- film grain
- lens blur
- chromatic aberration
- realistic skin pores
- camera flare
- overly photographic lighting texture

Use enamel highlights and stylized material shading instead.

## 8. Consistency Test

A successful output should satisfy all of these:

- the source image is still immediately recognizable
- the medium is clearly different from photography
- the same image could plausibly belong beside other Cloisonne Dream outputs
- no invented decorative object steals attention
- the gold lines feel structural and intentional
- the image remains visually clean at thumbnail size
